Portfolio Construction and Position Management

The Operating System

Three behaviors, one portfolio.

Portfolio construction is not asset selection. Asset selection answers what to own; portfolio construction answers how each position should behave within the whole. Parts 1 through 4 established the structural foundations — philosophy, thesis, backbone, wrappers. This part covers both building the portfolio and maintaining it: posture classification, position sizing, wrapper routing, and the governance protocols that preserve framework integrity through market cycles. Part 6 completes the framework with CIS scoring methodology and execution mechanics.

The framework employs three behavioral postures, and every position operates in exactly one. is controlled convexity: deliberate leverage placed on regime forces the practitioner believes are real, durable, and capital-backed, but still early, underowned, or mis-sized by institutions. Torque positions have nonlinear upside to structural drivers and real underlying businesses that survive even when prices misbehave temporarily. is capital-preserving exposure engineered to survive regime instability — positions that withstand liquidity stress, policy error, and narrative reversals without forcing portfolio liquidation. Ballast is rotation capital: trimmed after Torque runs, redeployed into Torque during drawdowns. is unbacked convexity dependent on narrative velocity rather than capital commitment. Hype positions capture momentum-driven mispricings under strict position limits and automatic exits; they can be tactically useful but are never allowed to become load-bearing.

Each posture exists because omitting it creates a specific fragility. Torque without Ballast produces fragility during drawdowns: concentrated positions correlate under stress, and without Ballast the choice is binary — sell Torque at trough prices or hold with no dry powder. Ballast without Torque produces stagnation: capital preservation alone does not compound wealth, and the framework’s objective is probability-weighted multi-cycle outcomes, which requires Torque capturing structural tailwinds. Hype without governance produces ruin: narrative-driven positions exhibit extreme reflexivity until the loop breaks, and the difference between speculation and gambling is governance — position caps, stop-losses, and classification discipline.

Bitcoin sits outside all three. It is not classified as Torque, Ballast, or Hype; it is the convexity backbone, governed separately under the Part 3 protocols, with its own -independent rules. The diagram below assembles the four elements into one machine rather than four labels.

Read together, the postures form a capital-allocation system rather than a labeling scheme. Torque converts thesis into convex exposure; Ballast holds the reserve that lets Torque survive its own volatility; Hype is quarantined optionality with an automatic exit; the backbone compounds untouched underneath. Each posture carries distinct sizing logic, holding horizons, drawdown tolerance, and governance — the subject of the next three sections.

Controlled Convexity

Torque: leverage on regime forces.

Torque positions are placed on structural shifts that institutions are underexposed to because of mandate constraints, compliance processes, or volatility limits. Return distributions are fat-tailed: a minority of positions may deliver multi-bagger outcomes while others underperform. Torque positions can experience 50 to 70 percent drawdowns during liquidity stress — that is the price of convexity, and position sizing plus Ballast manage the behavioral challenge. Typical allocation runs 3 to 15 percent per position and 40 to 60 percent in aggregate during high-conviction regimes.

Eligibility requires three simultaneous conditions; missing any one disqualifies.

  1. Structural tailwind from identifiable regime forces. The position must benefit directly from durable macro forces — not general beta or sector rotation. Part 2 established how to identify a regime thesis; specific forces vary by practitioner. Examples: 4th Turning demographic stress, AI infrastructure buildout, defense modernization, energy constraint, monetary disorder.

  2. Business model survivability under stress. Funding secured, customer concentration manageable, balance sheet adequate for 12–24 month liquidity droughts. Many small-caps offer exceptional TAM headroom but fail this test because they need continuous capital market access. Torque-eligible small-caps have reached profitability, secured non-dilutive funding, or maintain cash runways exceeding plausible stress periods.

  3. Valuation headroom sufficient for convex payoff. Addressable market significantly larger than current market cap — often 5× or more. A 500 billion dollar company faces arithmetic constraints on 20× returns. Torque skews toward small and mid-caps where headroom exists, though market cap alone neither qualifies nor disqualifies.

Eligibility earns a position entry; it does not earn a position size. The framework separates the size a thesis could ultimately justify from the size the evidence currently supports — and the distance between those two is walked in stages. The Earned Conviction Ladder below names the six kinds of evidence a position accumulates on its way up. It deliberately carries no percentages: how much capital any rung justifies is decided by the sizing table that follows, not by the ladder itself.

Sizing maps directly from scoring, developed in Part 6, through four allocation bands. Scores reflect composite conviction across convexity, risk, macro alignment, and execution; higher scores earn larger sizing, and the bands enforce proportionality without requiring manual judgment at every threshold.

Wrapper routing follows expected turnover. High-turnover Torque routes primarily to the Roth IRA, where tax-free compounding regardless of holding period maximizes the benefit of tactical rotation. Buy-and-hold Torque with expected holding periods beyond one year suits taxable accounts under long-term capital gains treatment, or absorbs overflow when Roth space is exhausted. Pre-tax accounts are viable tactically — during accumulation with decades until withdrawal, when the current marginal rate exceeds the expected retirement rate, and assuming planned bracket arbitrage or later Roth conversion — consistent with the wrapper architecture in Part 4.

Torque Governance

  • Earnings proximity. Positions entering earnings within T-5 must be trimmed to 3% maximum. Non-negotiable. Proceeds park in Ballast; rebuild post-earnings if the thesis is confirmed.
  • Momentum tripwires. Exit when all three momentum dimensions turn negative — even if CIS remains high. Capital preservation overrides conviction; re-entry is permitted when momentum repairs.
  • Drawdown tolerance. Hold through large drawdowns when Ballast reserves are adequate and momentum dimensions remain mixed or positive. Selling Torque at trough prices is a framework failure.
  • Review cadence. Weekly CIS review, monthly formal reassessment. Material news triggers immediate review.

Strategic Reserves

Ballast: the reserve that buys the drawdown.

Ballast is not dead weight and not the opposite of growth. It is the strategic reserve that enables aggressive Torque sizing — liquidity, stability, and rotation capital during stress. Ballast businesses fund dividends even when credit tightens and maintain operations without capital market access. Typical allocation runs 2 to 8 percent per position and 20 to 35 percent in aggregate, rising during risk-off and falling during high-conviction Torque opportunities.

The rotation function is what earns Ballast its space. Trimming Ballast after Torque runs and deploying it into Torque during drawdowns is the framework’s primary mechanism for buying low without raising external cash or touching Bitcoin. Distributions accumulate as deployment capital; during stress or Bitcoin dislocations, that yield funds accumulation without selling depressed positions. The cycle below traces the full loop — including the governed checkpoint that decides whether a drawdown is buyable.

Eligibility is criteria-based: a position must satisfy at least three of the five tests below. Yield can be a consequence of qualifying, but it is never the selection criterion — classification follows from stress resilience, and income follows from business quality.

  1. Fortress balance sheet

    Net cash or minimal leverage, no near-term refinancing, investment-grade or equivalent strength.

  2. Diversified revenue

    No single customer exceeds 15% of revenue, geographic diversification, no dependence on a single product cycle.

  3. Durable cash generation

    Consistent free cash flow through cycles, dividend history, capital allocation discipline.

  4. Low factor correlation

    Limited sensitivity to equity beta, real rates, and credit spreads. Moves independently of Torque during stress.

  5. Liquidity-resilient valuation

    Reasonable multiples with downside limited by a valuation floor, not a growth narrative.

Ballast CIS evaluates survivability and yield quality over upside convexity. Because the Convexity component carries 40 percent of the composite weight, capital-preserving positions — which lack the TAM headroom that drives high Convexity sub-scores — structurally score lower than Torque for equivalently strong positions. The same four-band structure applies, with allocation ranges scaled to Ballast’s role as reserve capital rather than growth engine.

A concrete reference: STRC exemplifies prototypical Ballast — a preferred instrument yielding approximately 11 percent as of January 2026 with return-of-capital treatment. The caveats travel with the example: yield varies with price, and ROC characterization is tax-year dependent, verified annually through issuer 1099-DIV reporting. Wrapper routing follows the distribution profile — taxable is the primary destination, especially for ROC-characterized distributions and tax-loss harvesting; pre-tax suits ordinary income generators such as bond funds and REITs with non-ROC distributions; Roth is generally inefficient for Ballast, since Roth’s benefits are maximized by high-growth positions.

Ballast Governance

  • Rotation triggers. Trim Ballast after Torque runs; deploy into Torque during drawdowns when momentum remains constructive. The primary mechanism for buying low without external cash.
  • Yield deployment. Distributions accumulate as deployment capital. During stress or Bitcoin dislocations, yield funds DCA without selling depressed positions.
  • Minimum reserve. Maintain at least 15% aggregate Ballast even during high-conviction Torque opportunities. Complete depletion creates fragility.
  • Quality threshold. Positions must keep meeting the eligibility criteria (3 of 5). Superior candidates replace inferior holdings rather than expanding count; Ballast is intentionally limited to best-in-class survivors.
  • Review cadence. Quarterly, unless material deterioration is observed.

Disciplined Speculation

Hype: quarantined, capped, and pre-committed to exit.

Markets occasionally misprice assets on sentiment extremes, regulatory changes, or technical factors. Some of those opportunities lack structural support yet offer asymmetric short-term payoffs when governed properly. Hype exists to capture them — with price driven by attention and reflexive feedback rather than fundamental value, and with no floor when the narrative breaks. A Torque position collapsing 50 percent can recover because regime forces persist; a Hype position collapsing 80 percent may never return, because the system never needed it. Typical allocation is 2 to 5 percent per position under a strict 10 percent aggregate cap, and many portfolios appropriately hold 0 percent Hype.

Misclassifying Hype as Torque is the primary catastrophic failure mode. A position is Hype — whatever its label — when it exhibits any of the following signs.

Price-attention reflexivity

Price gains drive attention, which drives buying, which drives price — without business momentum underneath. The loop can generate 500% in months and give back 90% in weeks. If no sustainable business improvement is identifiable, it is Hype.

Valuation disconnected from plausible scenarios

A 500 million dollar company with 10 million dollars of revenue at 50× sales requires assumptions that strain credibility. Can still be profitable as Hype; should not be held as Torque.

Funding-dependent survival

Pre-revenue companies burning cash with no path to profitability. When capital markets tighten, these have no path forward.

Visible but unpriced moat erosion

Competitive advantage degrading while price has not yet reflected it. Can work as Hype — ride the narrative until it breaks — never as Torque, because there is no recovery when reality asserts.

Hype Governance

  • Momentum is the thesis — and the exit signal. Enter only on technical breakouts with volume confirmation. Buy into strength, not weakness; there is no value in averaging into positions without a fundamental anchor.
  • Stop-loss protocol. Automatic exit at 15–25% below entry. No widening stops, no averaging down, no converting to “long-term hold.” Exit and reassess.
  • Sizing and cap. Eligible from CIS 50; 2–5% per position; no Hype position exceeds 5% under any circumstances; 10% strict aggregate cap.
  • Profit-taking ladder. One-third off at a 50% gain, another third at 100%, remainder runs with the stop raised to breakeven.
  • Time limit. Exit positions that have not worked within 3–6 months rather than waiting for the stop-loss.
  • Reclassification prohibition. Hype cannot be reclassified to Torque to justify holding through a stop-loss breach. Post-hoc reclassification to avoid loss recognition is how Hype destroys portfolios.

With all three sizing registers now on the table, one comparison remains. A CIS score measures position quality; it does not by itself determine capital. The same score commands different size depending on the job the position performs — a 70 in Torque and a 70 in Ballast are both strong, but they are strong at different things, and the allocation bands reflect that.

The shared caps — 15 percent default, 18 percent absolute — cut across all three columns. Posture decides how much a score is worth; the concentration limits decide how much any single name is allowed to matter.

Position Management

Governance through the cycle.

With construction established — postures assigned, positions sized, wrappers selected — the framework turns to ongoing governance: systematic protocols for earnings exposure, momentum monitoring, regime awareness, position additions, and thesis validation. Three protocols carry most of the weight.

Earnings proximity. Earnings announcements are binary events that can move a position 10 to 30 percent independent of thesis quality, and conviction about business quality does not predict the reaction: a company can beat estimates and decline because expectations ran higher, or miss and rally because the miss was less severe than feared. The framework treats earnings as idiosyncratic risk requiring sizing discipline — the timeline below walks a full-size position through the compression, the event, and the paths out the other side.

The core principle: any position entering earnings within T-5 trading days must not exceed 3 percent of portfolio value. The cap is position-specific, not portfolio-wide; positions without near-term earnings maintain standard allocation. In practice: a defense contractor at 10 percent with earnings in T-4 days is trimmed to 3 percent, proceeds park in the liquidity sleeve or Ballast, and the position rebuilds to target over 2 to 4 weeks if the thesis is confirmed.

Earnings proximity protocol by trading-day window
Timeframe Rule Rationale
T-21 to T-6Initiation blackout for positions priced for perfectionAvoid entering at maximum expectation levels
T-5 to T-1Cap the position entering earnings at 3% of portfolioLimit binary event exposure for that specific name
T+0No action; observe the reactionLet the market process information
T+1 to T+5Assessment windowEvaluate thesis impact from results and the call
T+6 onwardsRebuild eligible if thesis confirmedGradually restore position sizing

Post-earnings decision tree: thesis confirmed → rebuild over 2–4 weeks · thesis damaged → complete exit irrespective of loss, no averaging down · thesis uncertain → hold reduced until clarity emerges

Three exemptions apply: Bitcoin, which has no earnings and is governed by the Part 3 protocols; broad index ETFs, whose diversification eliminates single-name earnings risk; and Ballast positions at or below 5 percent where earnings are not thesis-critical, since the administrative burden exceeds the risk-management benefit for stable positions whose classification is not threatened by results.

Momentum monitoring. The framework evaluates three momentum dimensions before granting full sizing. Absolute momentum is distance from the 52-week high: within 10 percent is healthy, 25 percent or more below is correction, 40 percent or more is severe distress. Relative momentum is performance versus sector and market, distinguishing idiosyncratic problems from systematic stress. Breadth is the percentage of related names making new highs: strong breadth confirms fundamental support; narrow leadership warns of exhaustion. The gauntlet below shows how those three dimensions gate the capital a thesis is permitted to carry.

The action ladder is explicit. All three dimensions positive: full sizing per the CIS allocation table. One dimension breaks: reduce sizing 25 to 30 percent and monitor closely. Two break: watch status, minimal new exposure. All three negative: exit, even if the CIS score remains high. That final rung is a , not a guideline — a high-CIS position with all three dimensions negative has broken structurally. Exit, reassess, and re-enter when the repairs.

Three companion heuristics, borrowed loosely from Newtonian mechanics as metaphor rather than law, shape day-to-day application. Inertia: positions in motion deserve patience, positions flat 90 or more days deserve thesis reexamination. Proportionality: larger positions require larger catalysts — a 12 percent Torque position needs material fundamental change to justify a trim, while a 3 percent developing position can exit on technical deterioration alone. Consequences: portfolio construction is zero-sum allocation within capital constraints, so before any action, name where the proceeds go and which position is implicitly underweighted.

Momentum Overrides Conviction

Capital preservation and optionality take precedence over thesis validation. A position can be fundamentally correct and still destroy capital through extended technical deterioration; exiting preserves capital and optionality for re-entry at better prices, and re-entry is explicitly permitted once momentum repairs. The exit acknowledges that the market is not currently validating the thesis — holding incurs an opportunity cost the framework refuses to accept. Conviction without confirmation is speculation; the framework demands both.

Liquidity and correlation regimes. Position management adapts to liquidity and correlation regimes, not just individual price action. Elevated cross-asset correlation and liquidity withdrawal amplify Torque drawdowns beyond what single-name analysis predicts — positions that looked independent in normal conditions can trade as one position when liquidity leaves. The throttle cycle below shows the framework’s response as a state machine rather than a judgment call.

The stressed-regime indicators are VIX sustained above 25, credit spreads widening, and Torque positions correlating above 0.7. The response protocol: pause new Torque adds, enforce posture limits strictly, and consider reducing gross exposure by 10 to 20 percent. Resumption requires regime stress indicators to normalize for two or more weeks before standard positioning returns. This is regime-aware throttling, not market timing — the framework does not predict direction; it reduces exposure when correlation compression makes position-level diversification unreliable.

Add and freeze rules. Adding to positions requires discipline; uncontrolled averaging down and concentration creep are framework failures, not conviction. Positions down 20 percent or more from cost basis cannot receive additional capital without a confirming signal — a higher low, reclaim of a key level, or new thesis-supporting information. Scaling in occurs over weeks, not days: no more than 25 percent of target allocation added in a single week. The size gates repeat here for completeness: no position above 15 percent without explicit documented override, 18 percent absolute maximum regardless of override, no Hype position above 5 percent under any circumstances.

Thesis drift and invalidation. Price volatility and thesis failure require different responses. Each position carries one to three core thesis assertions documented at entry — for example, “defense budget growth sustains through 2027,” “datacenter capex cycle has three or more years remaining,” “management executing on margin expansion.” Violation of a core assertion triggers immediate reassessment regardless of price action; thesis-damaged positions are downgraded or exited, and price recovery does not rehabilitate a broken thesis. Momentum rules handle price-driven volatility; this protocol handles fundamental deterioration.

Known event risk beyond earnings. Regulatory decisions, policy announcements, contract awards, and insider lockup expirations receive earnings-style treatment: maintain a calendar of known binary events for each position, and cap exposure or hedge depending on posture. Diffuse macro noise — Fed meetings, election cycles — does not trigger position-specific action unless directly material to the thesis.

Regime Forces

Allocate by force, not by sector.

The framework allocates by regime exposure, not sector. Sectors are proxies for the structural forces in the practitioner’s thesis, and they matter only to the extent they capture identified forces. Part 2 established how to identify those forces; the specific set varies by practitioner — 4th Turning demographic stress, AI infrastructure buildout, defense modernization, energy constraint, and monetary disorder serve as examples throughout.

Under the example thesis, regime-aligned sectors include defense and aerospace (government customers, multi-year visibility, pricing power, strong balance sheets), AI infrastructure (semiconductors, datacenter buildout, power delivery — picks-and-shovels with order backlogs over speculative plays), energy infrastructure (traditional and nuclear power, grid modernization), and industrials tied to reshoring, where selectivity is required because many industrials are cyclical without structural tailwind. Regime-challenged sectors under the same thesis: long-duration unprofitable growth dependent on low rates (Hype if at all, never Torque), consumer discretionary without pricing power, and commercial real estate facing secular office decline and a refinancing wall. A different thesis would reach different conclusions; the mapping methodology is what stays constant.

Concentration in a regime force is intentional; concentration in a single sector is not. The discipline is to diversify within the force — to hold several economically distinct businesses that all monetize the same structural driver through different mechanisms, so one sector’s idiosyncratic failure cannot take the thesis down with it. The fan below makes that structure visible for one force; the example is illustrative of the methodology, not a recommendation of the sectors shown.

A practitioner bullish on AI infrastructure expresses that thesis through semiconductors, datacenter REITs, power infrastructure, and cooling technology — not 45 percent in one sector. The limits below hold that shape in place.

Conviction and Friction

Rebalancing as conviction expression.

Rebalancing is not mechanical. The framework rejects both calendar-based and threshold-based rebalancing, because both ignore the information content in price moves. A position up 50 percent appreciated for a reason; mechanically trimming it sacrifices winners. A position down 30 percent may have declined because the thesis is breaking; mechanically adding catches falling knives.

Rebalancing follows CIS reassessment, not allocation drift. On upward drift, recalculate CIS: if conviction is unchanged, let winners run; if conviction declined, trim. The question is “does my conviction support this allocation?” On downward drift, recalculate CIS: if conviction is unchanged and the decline is temporary, add; if conviction declined, reduce or exit. The question is “does this decline change my thesis?” The cadence is weekly CIS review and monthly formal assessment, with intra-week action only when a position moves 15 percent or more in a single week, the earnings proximity protocol triggers, a tripwire is breached, or material news changes the thesis.

Every rebalancing decision also has a tax dimension. Part 4 established wrapper engineering as the dominant structural return multiplier; turnover is where that multiplier gets paid or forfeited, because tax drag is a structural headwind that trading amplifies. The arithmetic is worth seeing once at full scale. Take 100,000 dollars, a 10 percent annual pre-tax return, a 30-year horizon, and a 25 percent blended rate on realized gains — identical asset, identical trades, in two different containers. Taxed annually, the effective after-tax return falls to roughly 7.5 percent; compounding tax-free, the full 10 percent survives. The curves below carry those two paths through time.

The terminal values are approximately 875,000 dollars in the annually taxed account against approximately 1,745,000 dollars in the Roth — a difference of roughly 870,000 dollars, about 2× terminal wealth, from wrapper placement alone. Arithmetic, not opinion. The canonical wrapper-retention exhibit — the Tax Wedge — lives in Part 4; this exhibit shows the same arithmetic compounding through time rather than at a single realization.

The wrapper-level rules follow directly. In Roth, rebalance freely: zero friction is the primary argument for running active Torque strategies there. In taxable, prefer new contributions over selling; when sales are necessary, prioritize long-term holdings and coordinate with tax-loss harvesting. Across wrappers, use rebalancing events to optimize wrapper placement as position characteristics update — a position whose expected turnover has changed may belong in a different container than the one it entered.

Change Governance

Doctrine, parameters, overrides.

Every framework decays the same way: one small unexamined change at a time. The ACF defends against that decay by classifying every proposed change into one of three levels before it is made. Doctrine changes mean framework abandonment. Parameter changes mean calibration. Override decisions mean temporary, documented deviation for articulated reasons. The routing diagram below makes the classification the first step of any change, not an afterthought.

What Lives At Each Level

  • Doctrine — non-negotiable principles. These define what the framework is; violating them means no longer implementing ACF. Three-posture classification for all positions · tax wrapper optimization as dominant structural return multiplier · Bitcoin as non-negotiable convexity backbone with separate governance · momentum deterioration triggers exit regardless of conviction · Hype positions require stop-losses and cannot be reclassified to avoid them · Ballast eligibility requires meeting hard criteria, not approximations.
  • Parameters — tunable thresholds. Adjustable for valid, articulated reasons while preserving framework integrity. CIS component weights (default C 40% · R 25% · M 25% · E 10%) · position sizing bands within postures (e.g. 3–15% for Torque) · momentum dimension thresholds (10%/25%/40% from 52-week high) · earnings proximity cap level (default 3%) · concentration limits (default 15% single position, 18% absolute maximum, 35% top three) · Ballast aggregate ceiling (default 40%).
  • Overrides — explicit user decisions. Conscious deviations from default parameters: intentional, documented, and time-bounded; articulating the reasoning and the expected conditions for reversion; consistent with framework objectives. The framework remains the baseline — overrides are exceptions, not evolution.

Silent drift is not an override. A threshold that quietly stopped being enforced, a stop that was “temporarily” widened without a note, a cap that no longer binds because nobody checked — these are the failure signature of a framework being abandoned while its owner still believes in it. The override mechanism exists precisely so that deviation stays visible, reasoned, and reversible.

Portfolio boundary and single-name sleeves. The portfolio boundary is the household aggregate across all wrappers — Roth, pre-tax, taxable, and Bitcoin cold storage. All concentration limits — single position, top three, top five, sector — apply to this aggregate, not to individual accounts; a position held across multiple wrappers sums to one aggregate exposure. Wrapper-level concentration is permitted when aggregate exposure remains within limits: a Roth IRA may hold 100 percent of its value in a single position without violating framework rules, provided aggregate portfolio exposure to that position stays inside the applicable caps. When a wrapper is intentionally concentrated in a single name, it must be designated and governed as a single-name sleeve — drift monitoring required, rebalancing or trimming required if aggregate exposure breaches limits, and the designation explicit and documented. Single-name sleeves are a governance structure, not an exception mechanism.

Scope

The sizing bands, thresholds, and examples in this part are framework defaults and illustrations, not recommendations for any specific investor. This framework does not constitute investment, tax, or legal advice. Consult qualified professionals for your specific situation.

End of Part 5

Part 5 established the three-posture framework for position classification, then detailed the governance that maintains integrity: earnings proximity caps, momentum tripwires, regime monitoring, add/freeze discipline, thesis validation, and the doctrine–parameters–overrides hierarchy. The framework translates to any market by substituting local proxies for regime forces while preserving the core principle — classify positions by behavioral expectation, not asset type, and govern accordingly.
